What is the right age for preschool in India?

The right age for preschool in India: what NEP 2020 says, typical ages for playgroup, nursery, LKG and UKG, and signs your child is ready.

In India, preschool usually starts at around age 3. That is when the National Education Policy 2020 begins the foundational stage. Many centres also run playgroups for children from about 2. There is no single right age, though: readiness matters as much as the number.

Below is what the policy says, how class names map to ages, and how to tell whether your child is ready.

What NEP 2020 says about preschool

The National Education Policy 2020 replaces the old 10+2 school structure with a 5+3+3+4 design. The first five years are called the foundational stage, covering ages 3 to 8, according to the PRS Legislative Research summary of NEP 2020.

That foundational stage has two parts:

  • Three years of preschool, Anganwadi or Balvatika, from about age 3 to 6
  • Classes 1 and 2, from about age 6 to 8

The policy also says every child should move into a "Preparatory Class" or "Balavatika" before age 5. That is from the government's summary of NEP 2020's salient features. It describes learning in this stage as play-based and activity-based.

In February 2023, the Ministry of Education asked states and union territories to make six the minimum age for Class 1. The Tribune reported the advisory. The same report described the aim of giving all children access to three years of quality preschool before that.

Admission ages and cut-off dates are set by states and individual schools. Rules also change. Always check the exact cut-off date with the school you are applying to.

Typical preschool class names and ages

Indian preschools use a mix of names. If Class 1 starts at 6, the years before it usually work back like this:

Class nameUsual age at startWhat it often looks like
PlaygroupAbout 2Short sessions, play, songs, first time in a group
Nursery (Pre-KG)About 3Routine, language, stories, early motor skills
LKG (Lower KG)About 4Early number and letter sounds, group activities
UKG (Upper KG)About 5School readiness, longer focus, early reading and counting
Class 16 and aboveFormal primary school begins

These are typical patterns, not rules. Some schools call the year before Class 1 "Balvatika" or "Prep". Others combine years or use their own names. Ask each school which class your child would join, based on their date of birth.

Signs your child may be ready for preschool

Age is a starting point. These signs often matter more:

  • Can manage short separations. Your child can stay with a grandparent or friend for an hour or two without lasting distress.
  • Shows interest in other children. They watch, copy or try to join in with other children at the park.
  • Can follow a simple instruction. "Put your shoes here" or "come and sit down" mostly works.
  • Communicates basic needs. With words, signs or gestures, they can show you they are hungry, thirsty or need the toilet.
  • Has some stamina for a routine. They can manage a morning out without becoming completely exhausted.

Toilet training is not always required. Many preschools help with it, especially for younger groups. Ask each school about its approach.

If your child isn't showing these signs yet, waiting a few months is completely fine. Children develop at different speeds. If you have concerns about your child's development, speak to your paediatrician rather than relying on a preschool's view.

Starting later: is that a problem?

No. The Ministry of Education's guidance puts Class 1 at 6 and above, and NEP 2020 plans preschool from 3. Starting at 3 rather than 2 fits that design.

What matters more than an early start is what your child does there. Play, talk, stories, music and outdoor time build the foundations. Worksheets and drilling do not. Our guide to what good early learning looks like explains the difference.

Daycare can start much earlier

Preschool and daycare are different things. Daycare is mainly about care during your working hours, and many daycares take babies.

If you are returning to work, your child may start daycare well before preschool age. Many centres then move children into preschool classes as they grow, often in the same building. Our guide to preschool vs daycare vs playschool explains how these fit together.

Once you know the age and type of care, the next step is choosing the right centre. Visit three or four, ask the right questions and, if you can, try a day before you commit.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum age for preschool in India?

NEP 2020's foundational stage starts at age 3, so that is the age the policy plans preschool around. Many centres also run playgroups from about 2. Check each school's own age rules and cut-off date.

What age is nursery in India?

Nursery usually starts at about age 3, if Class 1 starts at 6. Schools set their own cut-off dates, and the names vary. Ask the school which class matches your child's date of birth.

Is 2 years too early for preschool?

Not necessarily. Many children enjoy a short playgroup session at 2. But there is no need to rush. If your child isn't ready to be apart from you yet, starting at 3 is perfectly normal.

What is Balvatika?

Balvatika is a name used for the preparatory year or years before Class 1. NEP 2020 describes a "Preparatory Class" or "Balavatika" that children join before age 5. Schools use the term in slightly different ways, so check what it means at yours.
