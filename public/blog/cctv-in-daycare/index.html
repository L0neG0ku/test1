CCTV in daycare: what it can and can't tell you

CCTV in daycare explained for parents: live feeds vs recordings, who can watch, other children's privacy, and why cameras never replace good staff.

By The Kribble team · 24 October 2026 · 4 min read

CCTV in a daycare can show you what happened in a room after the fact, and sometimes what is happening right now. It cannot tell you whether the staff are kind, attentive or well trained. Treat cameras as one useful tool, not as proof that a centre is safe.

Many parents ask about CCTV first. That makes sense. It feels like control when you're handing your child to strangers. But the questions behind the camera matter more than the camera itself.

Live feeds vs recorded footage

There are two very different set-ups, and centres often blur them.

Recorded CCTV onlyLive feed for parents
Who watchesCentre staff, usually after an incidentYou, on your phone, during the day
Main useChecking what happenedReassurance, a glimpse of your child
Privacy riskLower, if access is controlledHigher: other children are visible too
What to askHow long is it kept? Who can review it?Who else can log in? Can it be recorded or shared?

Recorded footage is useful when something goes wrong. If your child comes home with a bump, the centre can check what happened. That only works if the cameras cover the right rooms and the footage is kept long enough.

Live feeds are a different thing. They can be lovely on a hard first week. They can also become a source of worry, as you watch a crying child and can't do anything.

Questions to ask about CCTV

On your visit, go past "Do you have CCTV?" and ask these:

  1. Which areas are covered? Classrooms, play areas, entrance, corridors? A good centre covers shared spaces and the entrance.
  2. What about changing and toilet areas? These should not be filmed. Ask how those areas are supervised instead, for example with open doors or two adults present.
  3. How long is footage kept? Ask for a number of days. If they can't tell you, the system may not be well managed.
  4. Who can view the footage? It should be a small number of named people, not anyone in the office.
  5. Can parents see footage after an incident? Ask how they handle this. A careful answer will balance your right to know with other children's privacy.
  6. Is anyone actually watching? A camera nobody looks at is mostly a deterrent. Ask whether the owner or a supervisor reviews footage regularly.
  7. Are the cameras working today? It's fair to ask to see the monitor. A dark screen or a dummy camera tells you something.

The privacy of other children

Every live feed shows other people's children too. Some parents are fine with that. Others are not, and they have good reasons.

A thoughtful centre will have a clear policy. Who can log in? Is access removed when a family leaves? Are parents told not to record or share clips? If a centre streams to anyone who asks, your child is on screen for those people too.

What CCTV can't tell you

Cameras can't hear tone of voice. They usually can't show how a teacher comforts a crying toddler. They can't tell you whether staff are trained, rested or paid enough to stay.

They also can't prevent harm. They record it. What prevents harm is the right number of adults per child, careful hiring and a culture where staff speak up. Our daycare safety checklist puts CCTV in its place among 20 other checks.

Better signals than a camera

If you want to know how your child's day really goes, these tell you more:

  • The ratio in the room. Count adults and children yourself. See our guide to teacher-to-child ratios.
  • How staff talk to children. Kneeling down, using names, calm voices. You'll see this in ten minutes of a visit.
  • Daily updates. A short note on meals, naps and mood tells you more than hours of video. Here's what daily updates to expect.
  • A trial day. Watching your child in the setting is worth more than any feed. Read why a trial day helps.
  • Your child's behaviour over time. Settling takes a while, but a child who stays anxious for weeks is telling you something.

So should CCTV decide where you enrol?

It can be a tie-breaker between two good centres. It should never be the reason you choose a weaker one. A centre with cameras and too few staff is a worse choice than a well-staffed centre without a live feed.

Frequently asked questions

Is CCTV compulsory in daycares in India?

Rules on childcare centres differ by state and type of centre, so check locally rather than assume. Whatever the rule, ask the centre which areas are covered and who can view the footage.

Should daycares have cameras in changing areas?

Changing and toilet areas should not be filmed, to protect children's privacy. Ask how those areas are supervised instead, such as open doors or two adults present.

Are live CCTV feeds for parents a good idea?

They can reassure some parents, especially in the first weeks. But they show other children too, and they can add worry. Ask who can log in and whether recording or sharing clips is allowed.

Can I ask to see CCTV footage if my child is hurt?

You can ask, and a good centre will have a process for it. They may review it with you rather than hand over a copy, to protect other children's privacy.
