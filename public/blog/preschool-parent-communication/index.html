Parent communication for preschools: daily updates parents actually value

A practical guide to preschool parent communication: what to share each day, how often, photo consent, incident reports and tools that save staff time.

By The Kribble team · 28 October 2026 · 4 min read

Good preschool parent communication is short, regular and specific. Parents want to know their child ate, slept, was safe and had a good day. They want to hear about any problem from you first. A few honest lines and one good photo usually matter more than a polished newsletter.

Here is what to share, how often, and how to keep it manageable for your staff.

What parents actually want to know

Parents of young children are handing over the most important part of their day. What they want is reassurance and a window into their child's life.

Our parent-facing guide on daily updates from daycare tells families to expect these basics:

  • Meals: what was eaten, roughly how much, anything refused.
  • Sleep: nap times and length.
  • Nappies or toilet visits for younger children.
  • Mood: settled, tired, unusually quiet, a hard drop-off.
  • Activities: what they did, and something they enjoyed.
  • Incidents: any bump, fall, bite or illness, however small.

If your updates already cover these, you are meeting expectations. The difference between good and great is in the detail.

Specific beats generic

Compare two example updates:

"Aarav had a good day."

"Aarav ate all his dal-rice, napped for an hour and a half, and spent ages at the water table pouring between cups."

The second takes 30 seconds longer to write. It tells the parent someone actually noticed their child. It also gives them something to talk about at home.

Consistency matters more than volume. Parents relax when updates arrive at the same time each day.

Extra care during settling in

The first two weeks are the hardest for families. A quick message mid-morning ("She stopped crying ten minutes after you left and is now playing with blocks") can transform a parent's day. Our guide to settling in at daycare shows what parents are going through.

Photos and consent

Photos are often the part parents love most. They also need the most care.

  • Ask for written consent at enrolment, with clear options: photos shared privately with the family, group photos, use on social media or not at all.
  • Respect the choice of every family. In group photos, avoid showing children whose parents have not agreed.
  • Use staff-owned or centre devices with care. Set a clear rule about where photos are stored and when they are deleted.
  • Never post identifiable photos publicly without explicit permission.

Parents who ask about CCTV in daycare often have similar privacy concerns. Have a clear, written answer ready for both.

Reporting incidents well

How you handle a scraped knee affects trust more than any brochure. A good incident report:

  1. Tells the parent the same day, before or at pickup, not days later.
  2. Says what happened, when, and where, in plain words.
  3. Explains what staff did: first aid, comfort, observation.
  4. Notes any follow-up: watching for symptoms, a change to supervision.
  5. Is signed by the staff member and acknowledged by the parent.

For anything beyond a minor bump, call the parent straight away. Then follow up in writing.

If two children are involved, such as a bite, tell both families. Do not name the other child.

Choosing a communication tool

Many centres start with WhatsApp groups. They are easy, but they mix chatter with important messages, share every parent's number with the group and make individual updates hard to find later.

When choosing any tool, ask:

  • Can each family get private updates about their own child?
  • Can staff post quickly, during a busy day, on a centre device?
  • Are photos shared only with the right families?
  • Can parents find past updates, receipts and notices easily?
  • Can you control who has access when a staff member leaves?

Communication as admissions

Families who feel informed tend to tell their friends. Good daily updates are one of the most effective ways to get more preschool admissions through referrals.

They also answer what new parents check for when they choose a daycare: will I know what happens to my child each day?

Frequently asked questions

How often should a preschool update parents?

Daily updates on meals, sleep, mood and activities work well for most families, especially for younger children. Add a weekly note on what the class is learning and termly progress notes. Incidents should always be reported the same day.

What should be in a daily daycare report?

Include meals, naps, nappies or toilet visits, mood and the main activities. A specific line about something the child enjoyed makes a big difference. Note any incident, however minor.

Do preschools need parent consent for photos?

Asking for written consent at enrolment is good practice. Give families clear choices about private sharing, group photos and social media. Respect each family's choice in every photo you share.

Is WhatsApp good enough for parent communication?

It is easy to start with but has drawbacks. Group chats expose parents' numbers, mix casual messages with important ones and make individual updates hard to find. A tool with private, per-child updates is usually easier to manage as you grow.
