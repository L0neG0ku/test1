Teacher-to-child ratio in daycares and preschools: what is good?

Teacher child ratio in daycare, explained by age: what India's ECCE policy sets, how international norms compare for babies, and how to check the real ratio.

A good teacher-to-child ratio depends on age: the younger the child, the fewer children each adult should look after. India's National ECCE Policy 2013 sets one adult for every 10 children under 3 and one for every 20 aged 3 to 6. For babies and toddlers, many parents look for far fewer children per adult than that.

Here is why ratio matters, what the numbers look like, and how to check the real ratio on a visit.

What a ratio means

The ratio is the number of children each adult is responsible for at one time. A 1:5 ratio means one adult for every five children.

It is different from group size, which is the total number of children in a room. A room of 20 toddlers with four adults has a 1:5 ratio, but it can still feel loud and busy. Ask about both.

Why ratio matters more for younger children

Babies need one-to-one attention for almost everything: feeding, changing, settling to sleep and comfort when upset. An adult can only hold, feed or change one baby at a time.

Toddlers are mobile, curious and quick. They climb, put things in their mouths and test limits. They need close supervision and a lot of help with routines.

Older preschoolers can play in groups, follow instructions and manage more of their own needs. One adult can reasonably look after more of them.

In every age group, a better ratio means more conversation, more comfort and more chance someone notices when something is wrong.

What India's ECCE policy says

India's National Early Childhood Care and Education (ECCE) Policy was notified in 2013. Its quality standards include these adult-to-child ratios, as summarised by Factly:

Age groupRatio in the National ECCE Policy 2013
Under 31 adult : 10 children
3 to 61 adult : 20 children

The same summary notes that children should never be left unattended. Treat these as a baseline, not a target.

How international norms compare for babies

Some countries set much tighter ratios for the youngest children. England's early years framework, for example, sets these for nurseries, as summarised here:

Age groupEngland's group-setting ratio
Under 21 : 3
2 years1 : 5
3 and over1 : 8, or 1 : 13 with a qualified teacher working directly with the children

These rules don't apply in India. They are useful as a reference point for what tight infant care looks like.

A practical guide for Indian parents

Putting this together, here is a sensible way to think about ratios when you visit:

Your child's ageWhat to look for
Under 1As few babies per adult as possible. Ask how many babies each caregiver looks after at feeding and nap times.
1 to 2Small groups, with one adult never stretched across a large group of toddlers.
2 to 3Smaller groups than the older classes, with extra help at meals and toilet times.
3 to 6At least the ECCE baseline, with a teacher plus a helper in larger classes.

These are not rules. They are a way to judge whether what you see feels right for your child's age.

How to check the real ratio on a visit

Centres sometimes quote a ratio that includes the principal, office staff or cooks. What matters is the number of adults caring for children in each room.

  1. Ask the ratio for your child's group, not the centre overall. "How many children and how many adults are in the toddler room?"
  2. Count for yourself. During your visit, count the adults actually with the children in each room.
  3. Ask who covers breaks and leave. If a caregiver is on lunch or sick, who steps in? A good answer names a floating staff member.
  4. Ask about the busiest times. Ratios often slip at drop-off, pickup, meals and nap time. Ask how they manage those moments.
  5. Ask whether helpers are counted. Helpers matter, but you want to know how many trained caregivers there are.
  6. Visit at different times. If you can, drop in once in the morning and once near pickup.

Add these to your list of questions to ask on a daycare visit.

Ratio is not everything

A good ratio with unkind or untrained staff is still poor care. A slightly higher ratio with warm, experienced, consistent caregivers can work well, especially for older children.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good teacher-to-child ratio for a toddler?

India's National ECCE Policy 2013 sets one adult for every 10 children under 3. Many parents prefer smaller groups than that for toddlers, who need close supervision. Count the adults in the room during your visit and ask how they manage meal and nap times.

What is the ratio for preschool in India?

The National ECCE Policy 2013 sets one adult for every 20 children aged 3 to 6. Many preschools have a teacher plus a helper in each class, which improves this. Ask each centre for the numbers in your child's class.

Does the ratio include helpers and nannies?

Some centres count helpers, and some don't. Helpers make a real difference, but ask how many trained caregivers are in the room as well. Ask directly so you know what the quoted number means.

What if a centre's ratio looks worse than it said?

Ask about it calmly. There may be a reason, such as a staff break. If the gap is large or happens often, treat it as a warning sign about staffing.
