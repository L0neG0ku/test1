A daycare trial day lets you see how your child, the staff and the routine fit together before you pay admission fees. A visit shows you the building and the manager. A trial day shows you how the centre treats your child on an ordinary day.

If a centre offers a trial, or lets you book a single day, it is worth doing before you commit.

## Why a visit isn't enough

On a visit, you see the centre at its best. Staff know a parent is watching, and you only stay for half an hour or so.

A trial day is closer to real life. You see how staff handle your child's tears, meals, nap and play over several hours. You also see what your child says and does afterwards.

It can save you from a costly mistake. Admission fees and deposits are often non-refundable, so a day's trial is a small step before a large commitment. Our guide to [daycare fees](/blog/daycare-fees-explained/) explains what you might be paying for.

## What to ask before you book

Not every centre offers trial days, and they work differently. Ask:

- Do you offer a trial day, or a short paid day, before enrolment?
- What hours would my child attend?
- Can I stay for part of it, or should I leave?
- Will my child be with the same group and caregiver they would have if they joined?
- Is the trial fee adjusted against admission if we enrol?
- What should I send with my child?

Ask for the trial on a normal weekday, with the usual group. A day with only a few children won't show you much.

## How to prepare your child

A little preparation makes the day easier for everyone.

1. **Talk about it simply.** "Tomorrow you'll go to a new place with toys and other children. I'll come back after lunch."
2. **Visit together first, if you can.** Even ten minutes in the room helps.
3. **Pack familiar things.** A favourite toy, a comfort blanket, a change of clothes and any food your child needs.
4. **Share your child's routine.** Tell staff about nap times, food likes and dislikes, allergies, and how your child is usually comforted.
5. **Keep the goodbye short and warm.** A hug, a clear "I'll be back after lunch" and then leave. Long goodbyes often make it harder.
6. **Keep the day short.** A half day is often enough for a first trial, especially for younger children.

Some tears at drop-off are normal. They don't mean the centre is wrong for your child. Our guide to [settling into daycare](/blog/settling-in-at-daycare/) explains what usually happens in the first weeks.

## What to watch during the trial

If you can stay for part of the day, or watch during drop-off and pickup, look for these.

### How staff greet your child

Does a caregiver come to your child, kneel to their level and say hello by name? A warm first greeting is a good sign.

### How they handle tears

Do staff comfort your child calmly and stay close? Or is your child left to cry while staff carry on? Ask how long the crying lasted after you left.

### Whether your child is included

Is your child invited to join activities, shown where things are and introduced to other children? Or left on the edge?

### Meals and nap

Ask what your child ate and whether they slept. For a first day, little sleep is common. What matters is how staff tried to help.

### How the room runs

Notice whether the day feels calm and organised, or chaotic. Count the adults and children. Our [teacher-to-child ratio guide](/blog/teacher-child-ratio-guide/) explains what to expect at each age.

## What to look for at pickup

Pickup tells you a lot.

- **The handover.** Does a caregiver tell you specific things about the day, such as what your child played, ate and how they slept?
- **Your child's mood.** Tired is normal. Relaxed, chatty or eager to show you something is a great sign.
- **What your child says.** Over the next day, listen for names of teachers or children, and stories about activities.
- **Pickup checks.** Did staff check your identity before handing your child over?

If anything worries you, compare it with our list of [daycare red flags](/blog/daycare-red-flags/).

## After the trial

Write down your impressions that evening. Add them to your notes from the visit. If you have tried more than one centre, a simple [comparison scorecard](/blog/compare-daycares-scorecard/) helps you weigh them up.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do daycares in India offer trial days?

Some do, and some offer short paid days or allow you to stay with your child for a session. Policies vary, so ask each centre directly. If a centre doesn't offer a trial, ask whether you can spend a morning there with your child.

### How long should a daycare trial day be?

A half day is often enough, especially for babies and toddlers. It gives your child time to experience play and a meal without becoming overwhelmed. You can try a full day later if needed.

### Should I stay with my child during the trial?

It depends on the centre and your child. Staying for the first hour helps you see how staff interact with your child. Leaving for part of the day shows you how your child copes without you.

### What if my child cries the whole trial day?

It doesn't necessarily mean the centre is wrong. Many children need a few weeks to settle. Ask staff how they comforted your child, and consider a gradual start with shorter days.
