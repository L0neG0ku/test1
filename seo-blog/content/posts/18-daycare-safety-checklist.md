A good daycare safety checklist covers five things: the people, the building, pickup, emergencies and everyday hygiene. Check all five in person before you enrol. Glossy brochures and a friendly owner are not enough.

Take the 20 points below on your visit. For the wider picture beyond safety, start with our [complete guide to choosing a daycare](/blog/how-to-choose-a-daycare/).

## The people

Staff matter most. A safe building with careless adults is not a safe daycare.

1. **Background checks for every adult.** Ask whether all staff have police verification. That includes teachers, helpers, cooks, drivers and guards. A good answer: "Yes, for everyone, before they start. We keep the records on file."
2. **A written child protection policy.** Ask to see it. It should say how staff behave with children, who changes nappies and how concerns are reported.
3. **Child protection awareness.** Ask how staff are trained on it. Under India's [POCSO Act, 2012](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/14753/1/pocso.pdf), anyone who knows of or suspects a sexual offence against a child must report it to the police. The person in charge of an institution can be punished for not reporting. A centre should know this and have a clear reporting process.
4. **Two-adult rules.** Ask whether a child is ever alone with one adult behind a closed door. Careful centres keep doors open, or use glass panels, where children are changed.
5. **Enough adults per child.** Count the adults and children in each room yourself. Our [teacher-to-child ratio guide](/blog/teacher-child-ratio-guide/) explains what to look for by age.
6. **Low staff turnover.** Ask how long the lead teachers have worked there. Familiar adults notice when something is off with a child.

## The building

7. **Secure entry.** The main door should be locked or guarded. Strangers should not be able to walk in. Notice whether anyone asks who you are.
8. **Safe stairs, windows and balconies.** Look for gates on stairs, grilles or locks on windows and high railings on balconies or terraces.
9. **Covered sockets and hidden wires.** Get down to your child's eye level. Check for open sockets, loose wires, sharp corners and heavy furniture that could tip.
10. **Medicines and cleaners out of reach.** These should be in locked or high cupboards. Not on a shelf in the bathroom.
11. **Fire safety.** Look for working extinguishers, a clear exit route and a second way out. Ask when they last ran a fire drill with the children.
12. **Safe outdoor play area.** Check the boundary wall or fence, the play equipment and the surface beneath it. Ask how often equipment is checked.

## Pickup and drop

13. **Authorised pickup list.** Only named adults should be able to collect your child. Ask what happens if someone new turns up. A good answer: "We call you first and check their ID."
14. **Sign-in and sign-out.** There should be a record of when each child arrives and leaves, and who brought or collected them.
15. **Transport checks.** If the centre runs a van, ask whether a female attendant travels on it. Ask about seat belts and the driver's verification.

## Emergencies and health

16. **First aid training and a kit.** Ask who is trained in paediatric first aid and when. Ask to see the kit, and check that it is stocked.
17. **A clear sick and incident policy.** Ask what happens if your child falls, gets a fever or has an allergic reaction. They should call you, write it down and share an incident report.
18. **Allergy and medicine records.** Ask how they record allergies and how staff know which child has one. Ask who gives medicines and whether they need your written consent.

## Everyday hygiene

19. **Clean nappy and toilet areas.** Look for a separate changing area, handwashing right after, and child-sized toilets that are actually clean.
20. **Safe food and water.** Ask where food is cooked, who cooks it and how drinking water is purified. Look into the kitchen if you can.

## What about CCTV?

CCTV can help, but it is not on this list as a must. Cameras record what happens. They don't prevent it. Good staff, good policies and alert adults do that. Read [what CCTV in a daycare can and can't tell you](/blog/cctv-in-daycare/) before you treat it as a deciding factor.

## How to use this checklist

You won't get perfect answers to all 20. What matters is how the centre responds.

| Response | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Clear answer, shows you a record or document | Strong sign. They've thought about it. |
| "We're working on it" with a date | Fair. Follow up before enrolling. |
| Vague, defensive or annoyed | Concern. Safety questions should be welcome. |
| "Nobody has ever asked that" | Ask yourself why. |

If a point worries you, check our list of [daycare red flags](/blog/daycare-red-flags/) for what to do next. Visit twice if you can, once at drop-off time and once mid-day. A [trial day](/blog/trial-day-before-enrolling/) shows you more than any tour.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most important thing on a daycare safety checklist?

The people. Background checks, a written child protection policy and enough adults per child matter more than any camera or gadget.

### Should daycare staff have police verification?

It is a sensible thing to ask for, for every adult who works there. That includes helpers, cooks, guards and drivers, not just teachers. Ask whether the centre keeps the records on file.

### What does POCSO mean for a daycare?

The POCSO Act, 2012 is India's law on sexual offences against children. It requires anyone who knows of or suspects such an offence to report it to the police. A daycare should have a clear reporting process and staff who know it.

### Is a daycare with CCTV safer?

Not automatically. CCTV records what happens but doesn't stop it. Staff quality, supervision and policies matter more, so treat CCTV as one point among many.
