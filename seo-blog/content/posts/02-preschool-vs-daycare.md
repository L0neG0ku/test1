A daycare looks after your child for most of the working day, with meals and naps. A preschool or playschool runs for a few hours and focuses on early learning through play. If both parents work full days, you usually need a daycare, or a preschool with an attached daycare.

The labels overlap a lot in India, so it helps to look past the name and ask what the centre actually offers.

## The short version

| | Daycare | Preschool | Playschool |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main purpose | Care while parents work | Early learning and school readiness | Play, social skills, first time away from home |
| Typical hours | Long day, often matched to office hours | A few hours, usually mornings | A few hours, usually mornings |
| Typical ages | From infancy, depending on the centre | Roughly 2 to 6 | Roughly 18 months to 3 |
| Meals and naps | Yes | Usually a snack only | Usually a snack only |
| Structure | Routine-based, flexible | Planned activities and themes | Loose, play-led |
| Holidays | Often open most of the year | Follows a school-style calendar | Follows a school-style calendar |

Ages and hours vary by centre, so treat this as a guide and check the details on each visit.

## What a daycare is

A daycare is built around your working day. It covers drop-off before work and pickup after, with meals, naps, nappy changes and play in between.

Many daycares take babies, though the starting age varies. The best ones plan the day carefully, with stories, music, outdoor time and quiet play. Others are closer to supervised care.

Daycares also tend to stay open through school holidays. That matters if your leave is limited.

### What to look for

- Caregivers who stay with the same group of children
- A sensible nap setup, with separate bedding for each child
- Freshly prepared meals and a menu you can see
- Clear updates on meals, sleep and mood each day

## What a preschool is

A preschool focuses on early learning in the years before formal school. Think language, early number sense, motor skills, art, music and getting along with others.

In India, preschool classes often go by names like playgroup, nursery, LKG and UKG. Our guide to the [right age for preschool in India](/blog/right-age-for-preschool-india/) explains how these map to your child's age.

Preschool hours are short, usually a morning. That suits families where one parent or a grandparent is home in the afternoon.

### What to look for

- Learning through play, not worksheets for toddlers
- A daily plan that mixes group time, free play and outdoor time
- Teachers trained in early childhood education
- A clear approach, such as Montessori or play-way. Our guide to [preschool approaches](/blog/montessori-vs-playway-vs-reggio/) explains the differences.

## What a playschool is

A playschool is the gentlest start. It is usually for younger children, often a child's first time in a group without a parent.

The focus is play, routine and social skills. Sessions are short, sometimes only a couple of hours.

In practice, many centres use "playschool" and "preschool" for the same thing. Ask what the youngest group does each day, rather than relying on the label.

## Combined centres: preschool plus daycare

Many centres now run a preschool in the morning and a daycare in the afternoon in the same building. Your child attends the preschool classes, then moves to the daycare section until pickup.

This can work very well. Your child stays in one place, with familiar faces, for the whole day.

Ask a few questions first:

- Are the morning teachers and afternoon caregivers different people?
- Is there a proper lunch and nap between the two?
- Does the afternoon have planned activities, or is it mostly waiting for pickup?
- Is the daycare fee separate from the preschool fee?

## How costs compare

Daycare usually costs more per month than preschool, because it covers more hours, meals and staff time. Combined plans are often priced as two parts added together.

Fee structures differ too. Preschools often charge admission plus annual or term fees. Daycares more often bill monthly. Our guide to [daycare and preschool fees](/blog/daycare-fees-explained/) breaks down every component, including the hidden ones.

## Which one does your child need?

Start with your work hours and your child's age. Then use this table.

| Your situation | Child's age | What usually fits |
|---|---|---|
| Both parents work full days | Under 2 | Daycare |
| Both parents work full days | 2 to 6 | Preschool plus daycare, or a full-day daycare with learning built in |
| One parent works part-time or from home | 18 months to 3 | Playschool, a few mornings a week |
| One parent or family member home in afternoons | 2 to 6 | Preschool only |
| Irregular shifts or occasional need | Any | A daycare that offers flexible or single-day plans |
| Preparing for formal school | 3 to 6 | Preschool, with or without daycare |

If your situation sits between two rows, visit both types of centre. Your child's reaction often settles the question.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is daycare the same as preschool?

Not quite. Daycare is mainly about care during working hours, while preschool is mainly about early learning for a few hours a day. Many centres offer both under one roof, so the line is often blurred.

### Can my child go to preschool and daycare at the same time?

Yes. Many centres run preschool classes in the morning and daycare in the afternoon. Your child stays in one building all day, which many children find easier than moving between two places.

### Is playschool necessary before preschool?

No. A playschool can help a younger child get used to being in a group, but it is optional. Many children go straight into a preschool class when they are ready.

### Which is better for my child's development, daycare or preschool?

Both can be good. What matters most is warm, trained staff, a sensible ratio and plenty of play. A well-run daycare can offer as much learning as a preschool, and a poor preschool can offer less.
