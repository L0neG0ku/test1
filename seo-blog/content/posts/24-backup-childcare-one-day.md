When your nanny cancels, your backup childcare options are family or neighbours, an office creche, a single day at a daycare, or splitting the day. The families who cope best set these up in advance.

Every working parent hits this: a nanny's family wedding, a festival closure, an unwell grandparent. Here's how to be ready.

## Your backup options, compared

| Option | Best for | Set up in advance | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grandparents or family | Short notice, sick children | Agree when they're available | Distance, their own health and plans |
| Trusted neighbour or friend | A few hours, school-age children | Swap favours, share contacts | Not a long-term fix |
| Another parent in your child's class | Older toddlers and preschoolers | Exchange numbers, offer to return the favour | Both families may need help on the same day |
| Office creche or tie-up | Weekdays, if your employer has one | Register early, check age limits | Seats can be limited |
| One-day or hourly daycare | A full workday | Find and check a centre before you need it | Your child won't know the place |
| Parents split the day | Half days, flexible jobs | Agree the rota in advance | Hard on long meeting days |
| Work from home | Older children, light workdays | Know your manager's stance | Very hard with a baby or toddler |

Most families use two or three of these. Pick a first choice and a second, so you're not starting from zero.

## Option 1: Family, friends and neighbours

This is often the most comforting backup for a child. It works best when it's agreed, not assumed.

- Ask now, not on the day: "If our nanny is off, could you help on weekdays?"
- Keep a small kit at their home: spare clothes, nappies, a favourite toy.
- Share your child's routine, allergies and your paediatrician's number in writing.

If you rely on another parent, offer to return the favour. Shared backups last longer.

## Option 2: Your office creche

If you work for a larger employer, you may have access to a creche or a tie-up with a nearby centre. In India, establishments with 50 or more employees are [required to provide one](https://www.pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?id=150473), either their own or a shared creche. Our guide to [creches at work in India](/blog/creche-at-work-india/) explains the rules and how to ask HR.

Register early, even if you only plan to use it as backup. Check age limits, timings and how much notice they need.

## Option 3: One-day daycare

Some daycares take children for a single day or a few hours. It can be the most practical option when nobody else is free and you have a full workday.

Because your child won't know the place, prepare:

1. **Find the centre in advance.** Don't search on the morning you need it.
2. **Visit once, ideally with your child.** Use our [questions to ask on a visit](/blog/questions-to-ask-daycare-visit/), at least the safety ones.
3. **Check the basics.** Staff checks, pickup rules and first aid. Our [daycare safety checklist](/blog/daycare-safety-checklist/) helps.
4. **Pack familiar things.** A comfort toy, a known snack and a note on naps and food.
5. **Share your details clearly.** Allergies, emergency contacts and who will collect.

A single day can also double as a trial. If you're choosing a regular centre, see [why you should try a daycare for a day](/blog/trial-day-before-enrolling/).

## Option 4: Share it between parents

Sometimes the best backup is the two of you: one takes the morning, the other the afternoon. Our guide to [dads and daycare](/blog/dads-and-daycare/) has practical ideas for sharing it fairly.

## How to prepare, in one evening

Set aside an hour this week and do these:

1. **Write your backup list.** First choice, second choice, and their numbers.
2. **Check your leave.** Know how many days you and your partner can take, and how fast.
3. **Find one centre that takes single days.** Visit it before you need it.
4. **Make a one-page child info sheet.** Routine, food, allergies, medicines, doctor's number, emergency contacts.
5. **Pack a backup bag.** Clothes, nappies or spare underwear, a snack box and a comfort toy.
6. **Tell your manager your plan.** It's easier to ask for flexibility before a crisis.

## When your nanny cancels often

If backup has become weekly, the main plan may need a rethink. Talk to your nanny about what's going on. Consider whether a daycare, or a mix of nanny and daycare, would suit you better. ## Frequently asked questions

### What can I do if my nanny cancels at the last minute?

Call your first backup: a family member, neighbour or another parent. If no one is free, check your office creche or a daycare that takes single days. Splitting the day between parents can also work.

### Can I send my child to a daycare for just one day?

Some daycares do take children for a single day or a few hours. Find and visit one in advance, so you've checked safety basics before you need it. Pack familiar things to help your child settle.

### How do I prepare backup childcare in advance?

Write a list of first and second choices. Find one centre that takes single days. Make a one-page sheet on your child's routine and allergies, and keep a bag packed.

### Is one-day daycare safe for a toddler who has never been there?

It can work well with preparation, but it depends on the centre and your child. Visit together first, check the safety basics and pack comfort items. A centre's team should welcome your questions.
