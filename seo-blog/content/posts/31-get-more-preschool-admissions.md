To get more preschool admissions, make your centre easy to find, easy to trust and easy to contact. That means a complete Google listing, honest reviews, fast replies to enquiries, clear fees and a visit experience that answers parents' real worries. Most of it costs time, not money.

Here is a practical plan, in the order parents usually meet you.

## Start by thinking like a parent

Before choosing a centre, most parents search online, ask friends, shortlist a few options and then visit. Many follow a checklist like our [guide to choosing a daycare](/blog/how-to-choose-a-daycare/).

Read it as an owner. Every question on that list is a question you can answer before a parent has to ask.

## 1. Get your Google Business Profile right

When a parent searches "daycare near me", your Google listing is often the first thing they see. Treat it like your front gate.

- **Name, address and phone** exactly as they appear on your board and website.
- **Correct hours**, including extended daycare hours and holiday closures.
- **Recent, real photos**: classrooms, outdoor space, the nap room, the kitchen. Update them each term.
- **A clear description**: ages you take, programmes offered, timings, and your approach in plain words.
- **Answer questions** posted on the listing, promptly and politely.

A listing with dark, old photos and no reply to questions quietly tells parents something about your communication.

## 2. Earn reviews, and reply to every one

Parents trust other parents. Ask happy families for an honest review at natural moments, such as after the settling-in period or at the end of term.

Never offer gifts or discounts for reviews, and never post fake ones. Parents notice, and it can damage trust for years.

Reply to every review. For a critical one, thank the parent, acknowledge the concern without arguing, and invite them to talk directly. Other parents read your replies more closely than the review itself.

## 3. Reply to enquiries fast

Parents often contact several centres in the same evening. The first centre to reply helpfully has a real advantage.

- Aim to reply the same day, ideally within a few hours.
- Answer the actual question first, then invite them to visit.
- Share fees in writing if asked. Dodging the question loses trust.
- Follow up once after a visit. Do not chase repeatedly.

A simple shared enquiry log, with name, child's age, date of contact and next step, stops parents slipping through the cracks.

## 4. Be transparent about fees

Unclear fees are one of the main reasons parents walk away. Put every charge in a single written sheet:

| Fee item | What to state clearly |
|---|---|
| Admission or registration | Amount, and whether it is refundable |
| Security deposit | Amount and refund terms |
| Monthly, quarterly or annual fee | What it includes |
| Meals | Included or extra |
| Transport | Routes and cost |
| Extended hours | Hourly or monthly rate |
| Activities and materials | Any annual charges |
| Withdrawal | Notice period and refund policy |

Our explainer on [daycare and preschool fees](/blog/daycare-fees-explained/) shows what parents are told to look for. Answer it before they ask.

## 5. Make the visit count

The visit is where most admission decisions are made. Parents come with a list of [questions to ask on a daycare visit](/blog/questions-to-ask-daycare-visit/). Prepare for them.

- Let parents see a class in action, not an empty room.
- Show the nap area, kitchen, toilets and outdoor space without being asked.
- Explain your pickup authorisation, sick-child and incident policies clearly.
- Introduce the teacher who would care for their child.
- Give them the fee sheet and your daily routine to take home.

### Run open days and trial sessions

Open days let several families see the centre at once, with less pressure. Trial sessions let a child experience a real day. Many parents now expect to [try a daycare for a day before they enrol](/blog/trial-day-before-enrolling/).

## 6. Build parent referrals

Your current families are your best marketing. Referrals grow naturally when parents feel informed and listened to.

Good daily communication does much of the work. Our guide to [preschool parent communication](/blog/preschool-parent-communication/) covers what parents value most.

You can also make referring easy: a short message parents can forward, or a welcome event where new families meet existing ones.

## 7. Get listed where parents are searching

Parent directories and childcare apps put you in front of families already comparing options.

Kribble, launching in Gurgaon, will list only centres its team has visited and checked. Partner centres will be able to take enquiries, admissions and fees in the app. Our guide to [how Kribble's partner programme works](/blog/list-your-daycare-on-kribble/) explains the process.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I increase admissions in my preschool quickly?

Start with what parents see first. Complete and update your Google Business Profile, reply to every enquiry the same day, and share a clear fee sheet. Then run an open day and ask happy families to leave an honest review.

### Should I show fees publicly?

Parents value clear fees, and hiding them often loses enquiries. At minimum, share a full written fee sheet with every parent who asks, including admission, deposits, extras and refund terms. Clarity builds trust before the visit.

### How do I handle a negative review?

Reply calmly and promptly. Thank the parent, acknowledge the concern without arguing, and invite them to speak with you directly. Other parents judge your centre by how you respond.

### When is the best time to market a preschool?

Many parents start looking months before the new session begins, while daycare enquiries come in all year as parents return to work. Keep your listing, photos and fee sheet current year-round, and plan open days ahead of the main admission season.
