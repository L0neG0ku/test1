Good early learning looks like play: children building, pretending, talking, moving and asking questions, with teachers who join in and stretch their thinking. It does not look like rows of three-year-olds tracing letters on worksheets. India's own education policy now points the same way.

So if a preschool's main selling point is homework for toddlers, look closer.

## What NEP 2020 says about the early years

The National Education Policy 2020 replaced the old 10+2 school structure with a 5+3+3+4 structure. The first five years form the Foundational Stage, for children aged 3 to 8.

The stage covers three years of Anganwadi or preschool, then Grades 1 and 2. That is set out in the Ministry of Education's [two-year NEP implementation report](https://dsel.education.gov.in/sites/default/files/update/Nep_2020.pdf).

The government's [explainer on NEP 2020](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?NoteId=154948&ModuleId=3&reg=3&lang=2) describes a shift toward play-based, multidisciplinary and experiential learning from the foundational years. In other words, play is the method, not a break from learning.

## Why play is real learning

When a child builds a tower, they are testing balance, counting blocks and learning to try again when it falls.

When children play shop, they practise taking turns, using words, handling "money" and solving disagreements. When they mix water and sand, they are doing early science.

Play gives young children a reason to talk, think and keep going. Worksheets ask them to copy. The first builds understanding; the second often builds only the look of it.

## What to look for in a good classroom

Spend at least 20 minutes watching a class in action if the centre allows it. These are the signs worth noticing.

### The room

- **Low, open shelves** with materials children can reach and choose themselves.
- **Different areas**: blocks, books, pretend play, art, sand or water, a quiet corner.
- **Children's own work on the walls**, with each piece looking different. Twenty identical paper flowers suggest the teacher did most of it.
- **Space to move**, indoors and out.

### The children

- Busy, absorbed and chatting, rather than silent and waiting.
- Choosing activities some of the time, not only following instructions.
- Moving freely between small groups, pairs and solo play.

### The teachers

- **At child level**, sitting on the floor or a small chair, not lecturing from the front.
- **Asking open questions**: "What do you think will happen?" rather than "What colour is this?"
- **Following a child's interest**, so a fascination with ants becomes a counting game and a story.
- **Talking with children**, using rich words and listening to answers.

## Play-based vs over-academic: a quick comparison

| What you see | Play-based classroom | Over-academic classroom |
|---|---|---|
| Daily routine | Mix of free play, small groups, stories, outdoor time | Long periods of sitting at desks |
| Early literacy | Stories, songs, rhymes, talking, mark-making | Tracing and copying letters, rote alphabet drills |
| Early maths | Counting real things, sorting, measuring, patterns | Number worksheets, writing sums |
| Homework | None or a simple activity to do together | Daily written homework for 3 and 4-year-olds |
| Assessment | Teachers observe and share notes | Tests, marks or ranks |
| Teacher's role | Guides and extends play | Delivers lessons, checks answers |

## Red flags of an over-academic preschool

These do not always mean a centre is poor, but they are worth asking about:

- **Worksheets as the main activity**, especially for under-4s.
- **Exams, report cards with marks, or rankings** for preschoolers.
- **Pressure to write early**, before children's hands are ready to grip a pencil comfortably.
- **Little or no outdoor time**, because "there's a lot to cover".
- **Fees justified by "syllabus completion"** rather than by staff, space and care.
- **Children told to sit still and be quiet** for long stretches.

### Questions that separate real play from branding

Many centres say "play-based" or "Montessori" on the gate. Ask:

- "Can you walk me through a typical morning, hour by hour?"
- "How much of the day do children choose what to do?"
- "How do you teach early reading to a three-year-old?" A good answer mentions stories, rhymes and conversation, not tracing.
- "How do you track progress?" Look for observation notes and examples of a child's work.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is play-based learning in preschool?

Play-based learning means children learn through activities they find engaging, such as building, pretend play, art, stories and outdoor play. Teachers plan the environment and join in to extend children's thinking. It covers early literacy, maths and social skills, just not through worksheets.

### What does NEP 2020 say about preschool?

NEP 2020 introduced a 5+3+3+4 school structure. Its Foundational Stage covers ages 3 to 8, including three years of Anganwadi or preschool and Grades 1 and 2. The government describes a shift toward play-based and experiential learning in these years.

### Are worksheets bad for preschoolers?

An occasional worksheet is not harmful. The concern is when worksheets replace play, talk and hands-on activity as the main way of learning. Young children usually learn more from doing and talking than from copying.

### How can I tell if a preschool is really play-based?

Watch a class if you can. Look for children choosing activities, teachers at child level asking open questions, and varied children's work on the walls. Ask the centre to describe a typical morning in detail.
